SolfegeApp User Manual

Learn to sing in key with do re mi and live feedback from your microphone.

Open the app

Quick start: your first note in 2 minutes

  1. Open the app and tap Let's go. For the setup questions, the suggested answers are fine: tap Use la-based, then Continue, then answer the headphones question.
  2. Find your range. Tap Start and Allow the microphone. Slide your voice down on "ooo" for a few seconds, then up. Tap Sounds good. In a hurry? Tap Skip: choose a voice type instead.
  3. Start your first lesson. On the home screen, tap Start under Next lesson, then Start again.
  4. Listen, then sing. You'll hear a few chords, then four notes: do, mi, sol, do. When the screen says Sing!, sing them back (any sound works, like "la"). Follow the yellow bars as they reach the line.
  5. See how you did. Your voice stays drawn over the yellow bars: green means you landed the note. Read the message below it, then tap Again to try once more, or Next › to move on. If it went by too fast, tap Slow.

Is it working? While you hum, a line should move on screen. No line at all? See Troubleshooting. It's almost always the microphone permission, or the wrong microphone selected.

Most people who believe they can't sing were simply never taught. This app starts with matching a single note, then teaches the syllables do re mi, and then real songs in a key that suits your voice. You're new at this, not bad at it.

1. Before you start

  • A browser: Chrome on Android or a computer works best. iPhone and iPad work too, through Safari or any iOS browser.
  • A microphone: your phone's built-in mic is fine. On a computer, a webcam or headset mic works.
  • A quiet spot: background talking or music can confuse the pitch display.
  • Headphones (optional): you can do all the early lessons without them. Later lessons that sing along with music and other voices need headphones, so the microphone hears only you.

Tip: when the browser asks to use your microphone, tap Allow. The app can't hear you without it.

2. First run

The first time you open the app, it asks four quick questions. You can change every answer later in Settings.

  1. Welcome: tap Let's go.
  2. Syllables in minor keys: choose La-based (recommended) or Do-based. See A little about solfège if you're curious. If you're unsure, pick La-based.
  3. Notes outside the scale: leave this off for now. Some songs use notes from outside their key. You'll still sing them; the app just labels them "outside the scale" until you choose to learn their names.
  4. Headphones?: tell the app whether you have headphones on. This changes what plays while you sing (see Practicing).

3. Your singing range

Next, the app finds the notes that are comfortable for you, so every exercise and song fits your voice.

  1. Tap Start.
  2. For about 6 seconds, slide your voice down like a relaxed sigh on "ooo", to the lowest note that still feels easy.
  3. For the next 6 seconds, slide up to the highest note that feels light and easy.
  4. The app shows your range, for example "C3 to G4". Tap Sounds good, or Try again.

Don't push. Comfortable is the goal, not impressive. The app ignores strained or wobbly notes and trims a little from each end.

Rather skip the test? Tap Skip: choose a voice type:

Voice typeRangeTypical for
Low voiceG2 to D4most adult men

You can run the test again any time in Settings → Test my range again.

4. The home screen

  • Progress · Free sing · Settings: the buttons along the top.
  • Next lesson: the skill you're working on, with a Start button. Once you've practiced it, a bar shows how many of your recent notes you landed.
  • Songs: the songs you can learn. Tap one to see its course (see Learning a song).

5. Practicing

Every exercise follows the same rhythm:

StepOn screenWhat to do
1. Find home"Listen: this is home."A few chords play. They set the key so your ear knows where "home" (do) is.
2. Listen"Listen…"The app sings the notes first, and the yellow target bars show where they are.
3. Get ready"Your turn…"Take a breath.
4. Sing"Sing!"Sing the notes as the target bars reach the line. Your voice draws as a line over them.
5. ResultsA short messageThe whole attempt stays on screen, so you can see where you landed. Tap Again, or Next ›.

The first exercise of each lesson is a warm-up called Find the key: after the chords, sing do, mi, sol, do back. It doesn't count toward your progress.

Speed

The Slow, Easy and Normal buttons set the speed: half speed, three-quarter speed and full speed. Slow practice is how everyone learns, so use it freely. The app remembers your choice.

With and without headphones

  • Without headphones: nothing plays while you sing, because otherwise the microphone would hear the speaker instead of you. The quiet background note stops just before your turn, and songs play their melody for you first.
  • With headphones (turn on I have headphones in Settings): a quiet background note on do keeps playing, and song phrases can play along with you.

To stop an exercise partway, tap Stop. ‹ Back returns to the previous screen.

6. Reading the pitch trace

  • Lanes: each horizontal lane is a note of the scale, labeled with its syllable (do, re, mi…). The gold lane is do, home.
  • Target bars: the yellow bars show which note to sing, and for how long.
  • Your line: it's green when you're within the target zone, and blue otherwise. The faint shaded band around each lane is that zone.
  • A faded line means you're singing in a different octave, higher or lower than shown. That's fine: it still counts.
  • Wiggles are normal. Natural vibrato and sliding into a note are allowed for.

8. Lessons and mastery

The core lessons go in this order:

  1. Matching a note
  2. Holding a note
  3. Steps (do re mi)
  4. Small leaps (do to mi)
  5. Big leaps (do to sol, octaves)
  6. Phrases and songs
  7. Minor keys and modes
  8. Notes outside the scale (only if you've turned that on)
  9. Singing with a backing track
  10. Singing with other voices

A skill counts as mastered when you've landed about 8 of your last 10 notes (80% of the last 20) on at least two different days. The Next lesson card on the home screen then moves on to the next skill. From "Phrases and songs" onward, you learn through the songs.

9. Learning a song

Tap a song on the home screen to open its course:

  • The song is moved into your key, so it fits your range.
  • You already know lists skills you've mastered; those parts are skipped.
  • This song will teach lists the skills it practices. The drills use the song's own notes, for example "This is the jump in bar 12 of your song."
  • Then you sing the song phrase by phrase, and finally the whole song.

Songs in order: Ode to Joy · When the Saints Go Marching In · She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain · Oh! Susanna · Auld Lang Syne · Amazing Grace · Drunken Sailor · Take Me Out to the Ball Game · Shenandoah · Danny Boy · and the layered finale, Shchedryk (Carol of the Bells).

The finale is sung in stages, with headphones: alone, over chords, with a guide voice, with a guide that fades away, with a second voice, and finally with all the parts at once.

During testing: some melodies are still draft transcriptions, marked "Draft transcription" on their page, and the songs have no lyrics yet. If a melody sounds wrong to you, please say so.

10. Free sing

Free sing is a sandbox: no exercise, just your voice drawn over the lanes. Choose a key, a mode (Major, Minor, Dorian…) and a voice range, tap Start, and sing anything. The message at the top tells you which syllable you're on, and whether you're a little high or low.

The small grey line at the bottom shows technical details: frequency, confidence, input level and timing. It's useful when reporting a problem.

11. Progress

For each skill you've practiced, Progress shows your first day next to your latest day: the share of notes you landed, plus how many notes you've sung over how many days. Mastered skills have a ✓. Week by week, the difference is usually bigger than it feels.

13. A little about solfège

Solfège gives each note of a scale a syllable: do re mi fa sol la ti do. This app uses movable do: do is always the home note of whatever key you're in. So once you can sing do to sol, you can sing that jump in any song, in any key.

Minor keys: La-based or Do-based?

  • La-based (default): the syllables follow the key signature. A minor song's home note is called la, and a Dorian song's is re.
  • Do-based: home is always do, and the minor notes get their own names: me, le, te.

Both are taught in schools around the world. Switching never resets your progress; it only changes the names on screen.

14. Troubleshooting

The app can't hear me

  1. Make sure you tapped Allow for the microphone. In Chrome, tap the icon at the left of the address bar to check.
  2. If the app shows "Your microphone is sending complete silence", it's listening to the wrong input, for example a loop-back channel or a VR headset's microphone. Pick the right one from the list it shows, or in Settings → Microphone.
  3. On a computer, Chrome can keep its own default microphone. Check it at chrome://settings/content/microphone.
  4. Sing a little louder, or move closer to the microphone.

I can't hear the tones

  • Turn the volume up.
  • iPhone: on older iOS versions, the silent switch mutes the app. Turn silent mode off, or use headphones.
  • Without headphones, nothing plays while you sing, by design. The tones play before your turn.

The sound crackles, or the line lags

  • Close other tabs and apps, and keep the app's tab in front.
  • Try the Slow speed.
  • Please report it (see For testers), with your phone model and browser.

The line jumps around when I'm not singing

The microphone is picking up other sounds: talking, music, a TV. Find a quieter spot, or use headphones with a mic. Scattered sounds aren't scored, so they won't hurt your progress.

15. Privacy

  • Your voice is analyzed on your device, in real time. It isn't recorded, and it isn't sent anywhere.
  • Your progress and settings are saved in this browser on this device only. Nothing is shared automatically.
  • Because of that, progress doesn't move between devices or browsers. Clearing the browser's data for this site erases it.

16. For testers

Thank you for trying an early version. Things that are especially helpful to hear about:

  • Does the line keep up with your voice?
  • Do the tones sound clean, or crackly?
  • Was any instruction or message confusing?
  • Any melody that sounds wrong?
  • Your device and browser, for example "Pixel 8 Pro, Chrome" or "iPhone 15, Safari".

If the microphone misbehaves, open the app with ?diag=1 at the end of the address, for example …/?diag=1. The app then reports technical microphone details (level, pitch numbers, the mic's name) to the test server, which helps the team see what went wrong. It never sends your voice.
